# Room 2000 — history and reasons

The full record of what was built and why, kept from the first chats (2026-10-03 → 2026-10-15). Read the parts you need; `CLAUDE.md` has the rules and the current state.


## Rule: style coherence first

Any asset or environment the owner asks me to compose for the room-2000 game must keep the same style as what has been built so far. His words (2026-10-10, sent mid-task while I was replacing the bedroom action figure and restyling the Macrofrost logo): "remember this, it's vitally important as we progress: any asset or environment I ask you to compose, you must keep the same style, coherent with what we've done so far. even when you clear the context, remember this, please. coherence at the base of everything."

Clarified the same day, after I had written this up as "two looks": "the game in the tv won't be replicated as a style, it will stay only there. as for the memory, focus on the rest of what we've done." So **the style to carry forward is the bedroom's**. The 1998 low-poly look of the game inside the TV (Zelpa) is a one-off that lives only inside that TV: never use it as the model for new rooms, props or environments, and only work in it when he asks for something inside that game.

**Why:** the game is meant to be published and sold; he wants it to read as one piece of work. He explicitly asked that this survive context clean-ups, so it must be re-read at the start of every session on this project.

**How to apply:** (my reading of what "the style so far" is) the realistic early-2000s look of the bedroom — PBR `MeshStandardMaterial` props built from the helpers in `js/util.js` / `js/props.js`, canvas-drawn textures from `js/textures.js`, real-world sizes in metres, worn and lived-in ("human touch"), the same lighting (now 16:00, from `js/daytime.js`) and post-processing (grade, ACES — bloom is OFF at his request), the same level of detail on small objects. Any new environment beyond the bedroom should be built the same way and feel like the same game. Before building something new, look at its neighbours (same file, same helpers, same palette, same level of detail and wear) and build it alike; compare old and new side by side in the browser before calling it done. If a request seems to pull away from the established look, say so and ask rather than drifting. See [[room-2000-fps]] and [[user-states-specs-as-facts]].

## Rule: specs stated as facts

The owner (room-2000 game) frequently describes how something should be as if it already were so, in the present tense, with no "make it" or "should": "the character is definitely shorter in zelpa", "the room seen from zelpa isn't pixelated, it looks normal", "luna jumps after us inside, she becomes all pixelated". These are specifications of the desired result.

**Why:** On 2026-10-09 I read "the character is definitely shorter in zelpa" as a complaint that the character looked too short, measured that the height was identical and adjusted cues to make it feel *taller*. The next day he wrote "and make the character shorter inside zelpa" — he had wanted it shorter all along.

**How to apply:** When a message states a fact about the game that isn't true yet, treat it as the thing to build. If the same sentence could equally be a bug report (it describes something that would be a defect) and the two readings lead to opposite changes, ask once in a line before doing either, or say which reading I'm taking. My interpretation, not his words: complaints from him usually come with an evaluative word ("too much", "looks like", "doesn't make sense"); bare statements are specs. See [[room-2000-fps]].
